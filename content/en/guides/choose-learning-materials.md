# Choose material and use dictionaries well

Choose a short source for your goal and try it before committing.

## Choose the purpose

For ongoing reading, choose accessible, interesting content. For a listening difficulty, use a short recording with a transcript. For email, use relevant examples.

## Try a short section

Read or listen briefly without a dictionary, then summarise. Shorten or simplify material when nearly every sentence needs help. Background knowledge, pace and density also affect difficulty.

## Follow the message or inspect a difficulty

In extensive reading or listening, allow missed details. For close work, choose one important difficulty, check it and return to the source.

## Check usage in context

Identify the meaning and part of speech, then inspect collocations, structure and examples. Make your own sentence and use reliable dictionary audio for pronunciation.

## Use support, then reduce it

Use captions, translation or AI when helpful. Keep captions to follow content, or listen first and check captions when testing word recognition. Then remove some support and listen, summarise or respond yourself. Check important information against the source.

## Complete a small cycle

Understand a short source, check one expression and speak without looking. Handwriting and typing can both work: recalling without the source matters more than the recording method. Keep a main source you can return to rather than collecting endlessly.
