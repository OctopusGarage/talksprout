# Evidence for listening, reading, and writing expansion

Research date: 2026-11-14. Companion to `skill-expansion-feasibility.md`.

## Scope and evidence limits

This is a source-based feasibility review, not a systematic literature review. No external model/API sessions, audio experiments, browser tests, or learner outcome study were performed. Classroom findings below motivate task design; they do not validate AI delivery or establish TalkSprout's effectiveness. Publisher abstracts and university repository records were consulted where full text was unavailable.

## Listening: teach a process, and control access to text

Vandergrift and Tafaghodtari (2010) studied 106 university French learners over a semester. A process-guided group practiced prediction, monitoring, evaluation, and problem solving; a comparison group heard the same materials the same number of times. The guided group did better on the final comprehension measure after adjustment for initial differences, with greater gains among less skilled listeners. This was French classroom instruction, not an English AI tutor trial. [Publisher abstract](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1467-9922.2009.00559.x).

**Design inference:** A useful coach should ask for a prediction, play a short passage, collect the learner's understanding, replay to investigate uncertainty, and only then expose a transcript for comparison. Merely asking quiz questions after audio misses part of this mechanism. Dictation can target sound-to-word recognition but should not be treated as complete listening comprehension training.

**Delivery limitation:** Prompt instructions cannot reliably hide text in a third-party interface. ChatGPT's current official Voice documentation describes hearing responses while following their text and notes that availability depends on account and application conditions. Thus an audio-first exercise must explicitly ask the learner to avoid visible text, and its effectiveness remains dependent on the chosen interface. This is a limitation on controlling a listening-only attempt, not evidence that captions are intrinsically harmful. [Official Voice documentation](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001274-chatgpt-voice).

A learner's own transcript is useful for diagnosing what they heard, but a supplied transcript is not evidence that the model accessed the original recording. Repeating a sentence through generative voice does not establish word-for-word replay. Unverified audio access must trigger a request for an accessible source rather than invented acoustic diagnosis. These are product requirements inferred from the copy-to-external-app architecture.

## Reading: comprehensible input and evidence-grounded answers

Nation's four-strands framework separates meaning-focused input, meaning-focused output, deliberate language study, and fluency development. Reading and listening supply input; speaking and writing supply output. The suggested balance is a curriculum framework, not four equal UI tabs, and should not be interpreted as proof that every app must allocate equal time to all four skills. [Author's university-hosted paper, 2007](https://www.wgtn.ac.nz/lals/resources/paul-nations-resources/paul-nations-publications/publications/documents/2007-Four-strands.pdf) (indexed extract available; direct full-text fetch failed during this review).

**Design inference:** Use a manageable passage, ask for its gist before explanation, then ask a factual or inferential question requiring a cited phrase from the passage. Give optional vocabulary support after the learner tries. An AI summary without a learner reading attempt is reading assistance, not evidence of reading practice. Exhaustive sentence translation emphasizes deliberate language study and should not occupy the entire reading experience.

Difficulty labels generated by AI are estimates: a request for a B1 passage is not calibrated CEFR measurement. Offer shorter/easier material and user feedback on difficulty. For user-provided authentic material, preserve an exact source text for answer checking; distinguish what it says from outside knowledge. Do not adopt one vocabulary-coverage threshold as a universal guarantee across genres and tasks.

## Writing: preserve learner production and test transfer

Van Beuningen, De Jong, and Kuiken (2012) studied corrective feedback with 268 L2 learners. Their reported accuracy gains occurred both in revision and in new texts at posttests one and four weeks later, relative to self-editing and writing-practice comparison groups. This supports distinguishing immediate correction from transfer, but it does not establish the quality of current AI feedback, broad improvements in composition, or equivalent results for English learners. [University of Amsterdam repository abstract](https://dare.uva.nl/id/d195ffca-61d9-4ee7-b36a-32130f65c885), [publisher abstract](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1467-9922.2011.00674.x).

**Design inference:** Ask the learner to draft a real message before seeing a model answer; identify a small number of high-value issues; distinguish errors from optional stylistic improvements; have the learner revise; then assign a new, similar task without copying. The small feedback budget is a usability hypothesis, not an optimal number established by the cited experiment. Track task completion, meaning, audience/register, organization, and accuracy separately. AI polishing on its own demonstrates a better artifact, not independent writing ability.

## Implications for a prompt-only product

Reading and writing can be piloted through structured text prompts without an embedded AI runtime. Listening is feasible conditionally through an external voice app or through a learner-controlled recording plus transcript. Precise replay, concealed transcripts, acoustic checks, and durable learning records need controls beyond a reusable prompt if they are to be dependable.

A pilot should separately measure instruction adherence (does the AI wait for an answer?), delivery compatibility (is the actual audio/text workflow usable?), and learner outcomes (does performance improve on an unseen task?). Static prompt review and authored role simulations cannot substitute for any of those measurements.
