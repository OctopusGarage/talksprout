# Evidence and editorial decisions for the English learning handbook

Reviewed on 15 November 2026. The handbook combines original learning suggestions with limited claims supported by primary research and official product documentation. It contains no external navigation, prompt dependencies or source footer. References and scope checks remain here for maintenance. Research and documentation checks are not provider/audio tests or measured learner outcomes.

## Learning principles

- **Balanced opportunities:** Nation's guide distinguishes meaning-focused input, meaning-focused output, deliberate language study and fluency work. Use the framework to identify missing activity types, not to prescribe four equal parts in every short session. The handbook's routines and examples are editorial applications. [Nation's public guide](https://www.wgtn.ac.nz/lals/resources/paul-nations-resources/paul-nations-publications/publications/documents/foreign-language_1125.pdf).
- **Extensive reading:** Sangers and colleagues' 2025 meta-analysis reports small-to-medium benefits across included outcomes. It supports sustained reading at an appropriate level, without proving a particular book, daily duration or universal progression. Keep independent reading alongside focused analysis, rather than turning every passage into exercises. [Original meta-analysis](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-025-10068-6).
- **Retrieval and varied cues:** Butowska-Buczyńska and colleagues studied learning foreign vocabulary using contextual retrieval cues. Varied cues helped under the reported retrieval conditions. This supports trying an expression in changed situations as a practice suggestion; it does not establish equivalent gains in spontaneous conversation. [Original PNAS study](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2413511121). Broader spacing/retrieval evidence and limits are in the earlier [evidence report](2026-11-15-english-learning-evidence.md).
- **Shadowing:** The 2025 review reports a limited and varied evidence base, including uncertainty about segmental pronunciation and transfer to spontaneous tasks. Describe shadowing as one listening/imitation activity, not a complete speaking method or a guaranteed accent intervention. [Original review](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/29984475.2025.2546827), [author's institutional research record](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid%3A0980cee4-6f49-41c6-8f5f-f48831caf807). Discovery/abstract information was available; the institutional full page was blocked to the research browser. No effect size was calculated or republished.
- **Learning styles:** EEF finds insufficient rigorous support for assigning a fixed learning style and matching instruction to it. Its evidence concerns education, especially school learners. The handbook allows preferences but avoids using a visual/auditory identity to exclude a needed skill. [EEF synthesis](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit/learning-styles).

## Product facts and usable techniques

The separate [ChatGPT and Doubao report](2026-11-15-ai-chat-learning-handbook-evidence.md) records official documentation for voice, dictation, guided study, custom instructions, files, context and memory. The public chapters teach feature names and task choices rather than fixed icon positions, pricing, quotas, named models or unverified rankings.

The short English requests in the handbook are authored examples. They ask for turn-taking, brief feedback, hints or a recap; they are neither provider-certified prompts nor claims that the product always follows them. Speech transcription is separated from acoustic feedback. Memory is separated from a complete learning transcript. Different clients and modes are not represented as interchangeable.

## Absorbing resources without a link directory

British Council LearnEnglish provides skill/level resource discovery; VOA Learning English offers lesson, podcast and vocabulary material. The handbook explains when to use these kinds of material and what to check, rather than presenting a wall of outbound links. Neither site's entire catalogue has been tested. [British Council catalogue](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources), [VOA Learning English](https://learningenglish.voanews.com/).

Local source screening and private note identifiers remain in the ignored working inventory, outside the public repository. Retain the useful ideas: concrete situations, interest-led input, speaking before seeing a model, selective feedback, contextual vocabulary, replaying difficult audio, and returning to an expression later. Remove duplicate personas, fixed repetition requirements, unsupported neuroscience, arbitrary retention rates and broad bans on translation or dictionaries. Private examples are not published.

## Current structure and compatibility

The current classification has ten companion guides and seven further readings. Companion stages remain preparation, practice and review. Further reading has four full methodology articles and three AI reference articles: live voice, dedicated chats or bots, and global instructions. Selected Resources was removed at the user's request. Methodology readings explain complete workflows with worked examples and adjustments; overlap is retained when needed for independent reading.

The dedicated-chat article has five independently copyable configurations adapted from the supplied notes. The global article has two copyable variants, including the user's strict English-input gate preserved as supplied. These are reference instructions, not executions or formal language assessments. The new blocks use the shared clipboard writer with manual selection fallback and stale-result invalidation. Existing coach prompts and their archived evaluation data are unchanged. Earlier provider and feedback URLs resolve to the voice article.

## Gemini documentation check

[Gemini Live overview](https://gemini.google/us/overview/gemini-live/?hl=en) and [Google's Gems guidance](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/15146780?hl=en) support the distinction between text tasks, Live conversation and reusable Gems. Gems are not directly available within Live; do not assume a saved Gem's instructions carry over. Availability varies by account, client and feature. The chapter avoids quotas, fixed interface positions and model rankings. Its English learning requests are authored examples, not real provider or audio execution evidence.

## Usage modes and configuration scope

The user-provided AI chat notes supplied role variants and English-first instruction examples. Retained concise conversation, gentle support, focused retry, vocabulary clues and English immersion. Removed ridicule, mandatory rewriting of every valid sentence, unsupported fluency scoring and claims that text labelled permanent system rules can control platform-level instruction priority or context compression.

The three approaches, now presented as separate articles, overlap: voice describes interaction; assistant and global instructions describe configuration scope. One-off task briefs and project instructions supplement the model without adding another top-level category. The voice article explains existing app prompts. The other two contain reusable bot and global instruction configurations.

[Claude personalization](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/10185728-understanding-claude-s-personalization-features) distinguishes account-wide and project instructions. [ChatGPT Custom Instructions](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/8096356-chatgpt-custom-instructions) describes account preferences; [GPTs in ChatGPT](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/8554407-gpts-in-chatgpt) states GPTs do not inherit account Custom Instructions. Current creation and migration access varies, so the app avoids promising a universal new-GPT creation path. Gemini's current Gems guidance also describes a transition to skills; the article uses Gems as an example and directs readers to the mechanism actually available in their account. These are documentation checks, not provider execution tests.
