# Participant task cards

These English cards are ready for a facilitator to read one at a time. Participants may use either app language and answer in their preferred language. Translate the spoken goal faithfully when necessary and record that choice. Do not give the navigation path. Read only the quoted goal to the participant; setup notes are for the facilitator.

## Opening script

“We are testing the app, not you. Please say what you are looking for and what you expect as you go. You can stop at any time. Use fictional examples and do not share private conversations or credentials. We will ask separately for permission before making a recording.”

Record consent and recording choice in a private session record before beginning. A recording is optional. Assign a participant code; do not put names or account details in the CSV files.

## Card 1 — Start a short practice

“You have five minutes to practice English about your day. Find a suitable practice option and prepare what you would use to start in your preferred AI chat.”

Facilitator setup: fresh browser profile on the home page. Let the participant choose the UI language. Start timing after reading the goal. Record whether they find a suitable coach, copy its prompt, and can explain where to paste it. A copied prompt plus a correct explanation completes the preparation task. Actual provider use is optional and recorded separately. Reload after the task and record whether the chosen UI language persists.

## Card 2 — Save and reuse a scenario

“Prepare a scenario you expect to practice again and save it. Leave this page, return, and start preparing the same scenario again.”

Facilitator setup: keep the participant's profile from Card 1. Record selected coach, scenario, format and pace without recording private background. Observe saving, recovery after navigating away, and recovery after reload. Completion requires reopening the same saved combination with those options intact. Do not point out saving controls or quick access.

Optional follow-up after the main task: “You now have several saved combinations. Find the last one, then leave the home page easier to scan.” Before this follow-up, prepare four valid combinations through the UI, with the participant's consent. Record this as task `T2b`, separately from unaided saving. Observe discovery of expand/collapse and whether removal is mistaken for collapse. Facilitator preparation is not participant success.

## Card 3 — Find a learning helper and a review route

“Find help improving an English expression, copy the instructions you would use, and then find how you would review what you practiced.”

Facilitator setup: retain the same profile. Completion requires finding an appropriate learning helper, copying it, and locating a review prompt. Ask where each prompt belongs. Do not require an AI account or claim that copying proves the AI response works. If the participant chooses the transcript review route and has no conversation, offer the prepared fictional transcript below only after recording that intervention.

Prepared material (authored simulation; no model/provider execution):

> Learner: Yesterday I go to a park and meet my friend.
> Coach: What did you do together?
> Learner: We talk about our work and eat lunch.

## Follow-up questions after every card

1. What did you expect to happen next?
2. What, if anything, was confusing?
3. How confident are you that you could do this again? Use a number from 1 (not confident) to 5 (very confident), or decline to answer.

At the end: “Which action would you repeat on your own, and why?” Do not suggest a preferred answer.

## Timing and assistance rules

Use the same rules for every participant. Start after reading the card; stop when the completion criteria are met or the participant stops. At five minutes, ask whether they want to continue or stop; record the decision and actual elapsed time. This is an observation limit, not a performance target. Record provider waiting time separately from navigation time.

Allow silent exploration. If help is requested or the participant is stuck, first repeat the goal, then offer one minimal hint. Record each intervention verbatim and classify completion as assisted. Use `independent`, `assisted`, `not_completed`, or `not_attempted`; do not turn an unattempted optional task into a failure. Record observable actions separately from interpretation and preserve contradictory accounts.
